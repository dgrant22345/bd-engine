The operating question

Which accounts deserve attention today—and why?

A database can tell you what exists. A useful business-development queue must tell you what changed, whether the evidence is relevant, where a relationship may already exist, and what the next action should be.

Start with a bounded target list. Without that denominator, “coverage” becomes an impressive-looking count that cannot tell you what is missing. With a defined universe, every gap can become a concrete research or follow-up task.

The method

A four-stage staffing BD workflow

01

Define the target universe

Choose accounts that fit the desk, geography, role family, or client hypothesis. Record a company identity and the best known public careers URL.

02

Measure source coverage

Separate recognized ATS hosts, ordinary careers pages that need discovery, invalid entries, and unresolved companies. Keep every denominator explicit.

03

Rank current demand

Compare recent role activity, hiring velocity, role relevance, and network coverage. A strong signal is useful because its components remain visible.

04

Review the next action

Use the evidence to research, request an introduction, prepare an outreach draft, or schedule a follow-up. Keep a person responsible for the final decision.

Important: a recognized ATS host is not the same as complete job coverage. It identifies a compatible source pattern; it does not prove role count, freshness, relevance, or successful retrieval.

Seven-day pilot

Prove the workflow on a small target list

  1. Day 1: select 25 target accounts with a clear fit hypothesis.
  2. Day 2: collect or audit their public career-site URLs.
  3. Day 3: resolve the highest-value source gaps instead of hiding them.
  4. Day 4: import your existing network and identify possible warm paths.
  5. Day 5: rank accounts using current role evidence and relationship context.
  6. Days 6–7: review the top actions, send only the outreach you approve, and record what happened.

Guardrails

Keep judgment in the workflow

  • Use only company, contact, and public job data you have the right to process.
  • Show unresolved sources and missing evidence instead of converting uncertainty into a confident score.
  • Treat assisted copy as a first draft. Review the facts, tone, recipient, timing, and channel yourself.
  • Measure conversations and useful next steps—not message volume alone.

FAQ

Common questions

Does a recognized ATS host mean every job is covered?

No. It is a compatibility signal, not confirmation that every role is available, current, relevant, or successfully imported.

Does this replace a staffing CRM?

No. This workflow helps decide which accounts and contacts deserve attention now. A CRM can remain the longer-term system of record.

Does BD Engine send outreach automatically?

No. BD Engine does not auto-send outreach. It creates assisted drafts and next-action context for review. You decide whether, when, and how to send outreach.
