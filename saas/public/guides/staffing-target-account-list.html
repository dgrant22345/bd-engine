How to build a target account list for a staffing desk

A target account list exists to answer one question each morning: where should the next hour go? This guide covers how to choose accounts, how many to keep, what to record against each, and when to take one off.

Published by BD Engine November 12, 2026 7 minute guide

Keep fit and timing apart

Two different questions get mixed together in most lists. Fit asks whether you could credibly serve this employer at all. Timing asks whether there is a reason to contact them now. Fit changes slowly and decides who is on the list. Timing changes weekly and decides the order you work it in.

An account that is a strong fit with nothing happening stays on the list and waits. An account with a burst of hiring in a field you have never recruited for is not a target, however active it looks.

Write down what fit means for your desk

Start from placements you have already made, not from companies you admire. Four criteria are usually enough:

  • Roles. The job families you can fill from candidates you already know.
  • Location. Where those candidates will work, including remote and hybrid limits.
  • Employer type. Size, sector, and stage where your candidates have been hired before.
  • Access. Whether the employer uses agencies at all, and on what basis, if you know.

If you cannot say in one sentence why an account meets these, it does not belong on the list yet.

Where target accounts come from

  1. Past clients and near misses. Employers who have paid a fee, interviewed your candidates, or asked for terms.
  2. Where your placed candidates worked before. Those employers hire the same profile.
  3. Employers of people you already know. Your own connections show where you have a way in. A connections export turns that into a list you can sort by company.
  4. Direct competitors of current clients. Check any exclusivity or off-limits terms first.
  5. Local employers that publish their own openings. A careers page you can read gives you dated evidence later.

Keep it small enough to know

The useful limit is the number of accounts you can say something specific about. For one recruiter that is usually dozens, not hundreds. A common arrangement is a first tier of around twenty accounts reviewed every week, and a second tier reviewed monthly. Choose numbers you will keep to; a list nobody reviews is only a directory.

What to record for each account

  • The address of the employer's own careers page, and whether its job board can be read.
  • The roles there that match your desk, with posting dates.
  • Who you know, their function, and how you know them.
  • What you know about agency use: preferred supplier list, in-house only, or unknown.
  • The last contact, what was said, and the date of the next one.

Record “unknown” where you do not know. A blank that looks like “no hiring” or “no contacts” will mislead you later. The ATS coverage guide explains what a readable board does and does not prove.

Order the list by evidence each week

Once fit is settled, rank by timing: recent openings in your job families, several roles in one function, a role that has stayed open, and whether you know anyone there. Accounts with both a current signal and a contact come first. The hiring-signals guide covers this ranking in detail.

Prune on a schedule

Review the whole list once a quarter and remove accounts for stated reasons: the employer has confirmed it does not use agencies, it no longer hires your job families, or you have had no signal and no conversation for two quarters. Keep a note of why, so the account is not added again next month by someone starting from the same idea.
