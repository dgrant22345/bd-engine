## 2. Follow-up post: useful notes

A contact name tells you who. A useful recruiting note tells you what to do next.

Three prompts I’d use:
- What do we actually know, and where did it come from?
- What still needs to be verified?
- What is the next sensible action?

That is the thinking behind recruiter notes in BD Engine’s People workspace: keep the context beside the person, while treating source details and your interpretation as different things.

## 7. Fifteen-minute feedback session

Use sample data by default. Ask permission before recording; written notes are enough.

1. Ask what they use today and which task is repetitive.
2. Without coaching, ask them to find someone and identify the next action.
3. Ask them to locate notes and return to the list.
4. Ask what the comparison and draft do—and do not—tell them.
5. Ask what would make them come back and what current tool this could replace.

Log observed friction separately from suggestions. Record completed tasks, help needed, stated replacement and willingness to try again. Do not count compliments as proof of willingness to pay. Do not publish interview comments as testimonials without explicit permission.
