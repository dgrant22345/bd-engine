# Alternative brand names — first-pass shortlist

Positioning: a practical recruiting and sourcing workspace. Prefer a durable product name; describe AI as a capability rather than making it the name. No brand, domain, or social-page changes have been made.

| Candidate | Rationale | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ShortlistLane | Most explicit fit: move from sourcing to an organized shortlist | Longer name; narrower than the full BD feature set |
| ScoutStride | Memorable, active; suggests sourcing plus forward progress | Needs a recruiter-oriented descriptor; could suggest sports |
| RosterTrail | Suggests organized people and preserved relationship context | Could sound like staffing schedules or sports rosters |

Exact-name and recruiting/software web searches on 2026-09-07 did not surface an obvious exact-name competing product for these three. This is a preliminary search observation, not trademark clearance or verified domain/social-handle availability. Check spaced variants, domains and platform handles, then obtain appropriate trademark clearance before adopting a name.

Suggested descriptor: “Your recruiting workspace.” Suggested positioning line: “Find people. Keep context. Move conversations forward.”

Other ideas were deliberately screened out or deprioritized:

- [RoleHarbor](https://roleharbor.com/) already offers job-search alerts and company career-page monitoring.
- [Talent Thread](https://in.linkedin.com/company/talent-thread) already operates in talent/design hiring.
- [ScoutBrief](https://scoutbrief.net/) is already an intelligence research workspace.
- [Sourcefolio](https://sourcefolio.uk/) already markets a sourcing/workflow SaaS product.
- [SourceLane](https://www.whatnot.com/user/sourcelane) has existing unrelated commercial use; a clean-sounding backup, but not first choice for distinctiveness.
- [Talvori](https://talvori.eu/) is already used by a language-learning application.

Recommendation: test ShortlistLane and ScoutStride with several recruiters before committing. Ask what they expect the product to do from the name alone, whether they can spell it after hearing it, and whether they remember it later.
