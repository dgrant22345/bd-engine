# BD Engine 30-Day Founder-Led Marketing Launch Plan

Status: working launch plan

Primary audience: independent recruiters and hands-on founders of small staffing agencies

Scope: controlled beta acquisition and learning, not a broad paid launch

Planning window: first 30 days after the operator starts the plan

> All numeric targets and thresholds in this document are **provisional operating hypotheses**. Reset them after the first 30-day cohort establishes a real baseline. They are not market benchmarks or forecasts.

## Executive Summary

- **Lead with one narrow promise:** “Know which target accounts deserve your next call—and why.” Describe BD Engine as a hiring-signal prioritization workspace, not another contact database or an autonomous outreach tool.
- **Use founder-led LinkedIn and permission-based industry communities first.** Publish useful, point-of-view content, recruit design partners personally, and use the free ATS audit as the primary low-friction proof asset. The 2025 Edelman–LinkedIn study says strong thought leadership can help lesser-known B2B vendors earn internal advocates, but it is platform-sponsored research and should inform—not dictate—the plan ([Edelman, 2025](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2025-b2b-thought-leadership-report)).
- **Optimize for activated recruiter workspaces, not reach.** The instrumented seven-day threshold is setup + first target + usable signal + first workflow action. For marketing decisions, filter that cohort to recruiter-persona workspaces using real customer data. Likes, impressions, community member counts, and raw trial signups are diagnostics only.
- **Keep the launch controlled.** The repository’s current readiness assessment says limited beta, not broad paid launch. Do not buy media or promise production-grade support until the operational, email, legal, billing, and recovery gates in [commercial launch readiness](commercial-launch-readiness-2026-07-18.md) are complete.

## 1. The Market Wedge

### Ideal first customer

Focus the first month on one segment:

- Independent recruiter or owner-operator of a staffing agency
- Roughly one to five active business-development users
- Already maintains a target-company spreadsheet, CRM list, or LinkedIn network
- Manually checks careers pages or job boards for demand signals
- Does their own outreach and follow-up, so a better daily priority list changes behavior quickly
- Comfortable testing an early-access workflow and giving direct feedback

Do not optimize the first campaign for enterprise talent acquisition teams, high-volume sales development teams, or job seekers. Those segments have different buying triggers and should receive separate campaigns after the recruiter wedge is repeatable.

### Positioning

**Category:** Hiring-signal prioritization workspace for staffing business development.

**Core promise:** Know which target accounts deserve your next call—and why.

**Reason to believe:** BD Engine combines compatible public ATS activity, a recruiter’s own company/contact context, explainable ranking, outreach preparation, and commercial follow-up in one daily workflow.

**Primary proof offer:** Audit up to 50 public careers URLs in the browser, see the valid-URL denominator, flag uncertain sites, and export the results.

**Primary call to action:** Run the free ATS audit.

**Secondary calls to action:** Explore the live demo; request a 20-minute signal clinic; start the 14-day trial.

### Message hierarchy

Use this order on posts, calls, partner pages, and community sessions:

1. **Outcome:** Pick the most timely account and next action.
2. **Problem:** Target spreadsheets and occasional careers-page checks do not explain where to spend today’s attention.
3. **Mechanism:** Public hiring activity + the recruiter’s own relationship context + an explainable priority score.
4. **Proof:** A bounded ATS audit with an explicit denominator and export.
5. **Truthful constraint:** Recognized hosts do not guarantee role count, freshness, or complete employer coverage.

Avoid category language such as “AI lead generation,” “intent data,” or “sales automation” in the headline. Those terms imply capabilities and competitive sets broader than the product’s strongest current workflow.

### Claim-safe copy bank

Use:

- “Prioritize target accounts using current public hiring activity.”
- “See why an account moved up before you reach out.”
- “Recognizes supported public ATS hosts and flags uncertain sites for review.”
- “Uses your own target and contact context.”
- “Draft outreach from verified role and account context, then review it before sending.”

Do not use:

- “Find every open role.”
- “Real-time hiring intent.”
- “Guaranteed client leads.”
- “Automates LinkedIn outreach.”
- “Complete ATS coverage.”
- “AI finds hidden jobs or decision-makers.”

## 2. The First 30 Days

### Operating cadence

| Week | Theme | Founder actions | Intended learning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Prove the problem | Publish three point-of-view posts; interview five recruiters; offer three private ATS audits | Which language makes the spreadsheet/manual-check problem feel urgent? |
| 2 | Prove the workflow | Publish a short product walkthrough and two practical frameworks; onboard three to five design partners | Can a recruiter reach a useful ranked account with their own data? |
| 3 | Prove trust | Share anonymized findings, limitations, and one consented customer story; run a community signal clinic | Which evidence reduces skepticism without overclaiming coverage? |
| 4 | Prove repeat use | Publish what changed based on feedback; review cohort activity; invite only the best-fit users into the next cohort | Do activated users return and record a second useful action? |

Provisional weekly founder routine:

- 45–60 minutes each weekday for marketing and customer learning
- 15 minutes contributing substantive comments to relevant LinkedIn discussions
- 15 minutes on permission-based follow-up with warm or engaged prospects
- 20 minutes on interviews, onboarding, or turning one customer question into content
- 10 minutes logging source, next step, consent state, and outcome
- One 45-minute weekly funnel and message review

### Day 0–2 launch checklist

- Confirm all public claims against the current product and [README workflow](../README.md#product-workflow).
- Prepare one 60–90 second screen recording using synthetic data: import targets → resolve hiring signal → inspect explanation → draft next action.
- Prepare one redacted ATS-audit screenshot with a visible valid-URL denominator.
- Create the canonical UTM links in Section 7 and test that source/campaign appear in the existing analytics view.
- Create a lightweight design-partner tracker with consent status, source, segment, interview date, activation state, next step, and testimonial permission.
- Draft a five-question interview guide: current targeting method, weekly time spent, missed-signal example, last account prioritized, and what would make the workflow worth paying for.
- Confirm support ownership and the launch gates in [commercial launch checklist](commercial-launch-checklist.md) before inviting anyone beyond a controlled cohort.

#### Post 6 — A five-part hiring-signal check

> Before using a job posting as a recruiter BD signal, check five things:

> 2. **Freshness:** Is the posting current enough to inform outreach?
> 3. **Pattern:** Is this one replacement role or a cluster suggesting sustained demand?
> 4. **Relationship:** Is there a relevant person or warm path already in your network?
> 5. **Next action:** Can you turn the evidence into a specific, honest reason to contact them?

### SEO definition of done

Every published page should include:

- A descriptive title and first paragraph that answers the search intent
- Named author/reviewer and a meaningful “last reviewed” date
- Original screenshots or examples generated by the team
- A “what this cannot tell you” section near the relevant claim
- Internal links to the ATS audit, demo, trust pages, and one related resource
- One primary CTA, not a wall of competing buttons
- Accessible headings, alt text, canonical URL, metadata, and inclusion in the sitemap
- A measurement note in the content tracker: indexed date, query theme, impressions, qualified visits, audit completions, and activated workspaces where attribution is available

Do not mass-produce one thin page per ATS provider. Start with a strong comparison/explainer, then split a provider page only when search queries and first-hand evidence justify it.

## 6. Discord: Delay the Server Until There Is a Community Job

Discord should support retained customers, not manufacture an audience from zero.

### Provisional launch gate

Create an invite-only BD Engine server only when all of the following are true:

- At least 15 activated recruiter workspaces exist
- At least 10 users explicitly ask for peer discussion, office hours, or shared signal review
- One named moderator can respond on business days and run two programmed sessions per month for eight weeks
- Community rules, incident escalation, privacy boundaries, and the support handoff are written
- The product’s P0 support, recovery-email, and trust gates are complete enough that a community does not become the only support channel

If the gate is not met, use scheduled small-group calls and an opt-in email recap instead.

### Initial server structure after the gate

Keep the visible structure small:

- `#start-here` — purpose, affiliation, rules, privacy warning, and how to get support
- `#announcements` — releases, office hours, and incident links; no daily promotion
- `#introductions` — desk/niche, market, and what the member wants to improve
- `#weekly-signal-clinic` — volunteered public examples and scheduled sessions
- `#wins-and-learnings` — member-owned lessons; no unverified outcome claims
- `ATS coverage help` Forum — tags for Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, other, and needs-review
- `Workflow feedback` Forum — tags for onboarding, ranking, outreach, outcomes, and bug
- `Office hours` voice channel — scheduled, not permanently staffed

Discord says Forum channels organize longer discussions into searchable posts and tags, and they require a Community-enabled server ([Discord Forum Channels FAQ](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/6208479917079-Forum-Channels-FAQ)). If Community Onboarding is enabled later, use a small set of useful defaults and short role questions; Discord warns that too many channels or choices can overwhelm new members ([Discord Community Onboarding FAQ](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/11074987197975-Community-Onboarding-FAQ)).

### Discord rules

- Never request or permit uploads of LinkedIn exports, customer contact lists, candidate personal data, credentials, or confidential job orders.
- Use synthetic or explicitly volunteered public examples in clinics.
- No unsolicited direct messages, lead harvesting, recruiting pitches, or self-promotion outside a designated thread.
- Product support tickets, security disclosures, deletion requests, billing issues, and account recovery must use the official support path—not a public channel.
- State moderation hours and response expectations; do not imply 24/7 support.
- Remove unverifiable “guaranteed placement,” “guaranteed lead,” and confidential-client claims.
- Ask before recording a voice session and obtain explicit permission before publishing any excerpt.

### Current instrumentation boundary

The current landing and ATS-checker flows preserve privacy-bounded first-touch and last-non-direct attribution. Each touch can retain sanitized source, medium, campaign, content label, approved landing path, and persona. Signup milestones persist those dimensions, while the compact public/source summary still uses `source.campaign`.

When a person arrives from LinkedIn, completes the ATS audit, and signs up from the result, first touch can remain the upstream LinkedIn campaign while last non-direct touch becomes `ats-checker.coverage_audit_result`. That supports first-touch and assisted interpretation without storing a person’s identity in a UTM field.

Therefore, first-month reporting should:

- Use source/campaign for public page and ATS-audit traffic
- Use first-touch dimensions to describe acquisition and last-non-direct dimensions to describe the final assist
- Treat `ats-checker.coverage_audit_result` as the audit assist rather than erasing the upstream first touch
- Use the design-partner tracker to reconcile content-level conversations and consented interviews with product cohorts
- Avoid person-level journey claims: the implementation intentionally stores bounded campaign metadata, not an identity graph
- Never put a person’s name, company, email, role, or private community name into UTM values

### Minimal weekly source report

For each source/campaign, record:

- Unique public visitors
- ATS audits completed
- Signup workspaces
- Qualified recruiter conversations
- Activated recruiter workspaces
- Paid starts, if any
- One sentence on what message or objection appeared repeatedly

Do not combine anonymous public visitors and workspace counts into a conversion rate unless the linkage and denominator are verified.

### Day-14 decision

**Continue the message** if at least five qualified ICP conversations repeat the same prioritization problem and at least two users complete a real-data workflow.

**Revise the message or ICP** if conversations happen but the pain is consistently described as CRM hygiene, contact discovery, or outbound automation rather than target prioritization.

**Do not increase outreach volume** if fewer than five qualified conversations result after the founder has made at least 50 relevant, manual, permission-based interactions. Review the profile, hook, proof asset, and target definition first.

### Day-30 decision

**Go to a second controlled cohort** when:

- Five or more recruiter workspaces activate
- At least three activated workspaces record retained value in days 15–30
- Two users independently ask to continue, refer a peer, or approve a specific testimonial
- No unresolved material privacy, trust, billing, or support incident exists
- The P0 production launch gates have named owners and credible completion dates

**Continue learning without scaling** when two to four workspaces activate and interviews reveal one consistent, fixable workflow blocker.

**Pause or reposition the campaign** when fewer than two workspaces activate after at least 20 qualified interviews and hands-on onboarding attempts, or when the primary requested product is repeatedly outside the current scope.

### Paid-acquisition gate

Do not run broad LinkedIn, Reddit, or search ads during this baseline month. At the current $10/month recruiter plan, unknown retention and support costs make paid economics unproven.

Consider a capped retargeting test only after:

- At least 20 qualified recruiter workspaces have completed a full activation window
- Week-four retained value is measured and at least 40% for the target cohort
- Trial-to-paid conversion and gross retention are known
- A maximum acceptable CAC and payback period are approved from actual contribution margin
- Production billing, email recovery, support, legal, and backup/recovery gates are complete

## 9. Compliance and Trust Guardrails

These are operating guardrails, not legal advice. Qualified counsel should review the launch program and the product’s Terms/Privacy before broad commercial promotion.

### Messaging and consent

- No scraped email lists, purchased contact lists, LinkedIn message automation, connection automation, or community-member harvesting.
- For commercial electronic messages sent from Canada or to recipients covered by Canadian law, maintain evidence of consent or a reviewed basis for implied consent, identify the sender, and provide a working unsubscribe mechanism. The CRTC describes consent, identification, and unsubscribe as the three general CASL requirements ([CRTC CASL FAQ](https://www.crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm)).
- A publicly listed business email is not blanket permission. If relying on conspicuous publication, document where it was found, confirm there was no statement against messages, and ensure the message is relevant to the person’s business role ([CRTC guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm)).
- Honour opt-outs across email, direct outreach, and partner lists promptly; do not re-add people through another channel.
- Do not send product promotion through a recruitment message or job opportunity as a workaround.

### Data and proof

- Use synthetic data by default in public demos and content.
- Obtain written, specific permission for every customer name, logo, quote, screenshot, metric, recording, and case study.
- Redact company names, contact names, URLs, job-order details, and network exports from working screenshots unless explicitly approved.
- Never upload a prospect’s LinkedIn export, candidate list, CRM export, or customer record into a public community or third-party content tool.
- Keep counts tied to their denominator and sample. Label one-user and one-audit examples; do not present them as benchmarks.

### Platform and community conduct

- Disclose the BD Engine affiliation whenever the founder discusses or links the product.
- Read each group’s rules and request moderator approval before sharing a tool, event, or case study.
- Do not imitate independent praise, seed undisclosed testimonials, coordinate engagement, or use alternate accounts.
- Do not ask community members to direct-message personal data. Route account-specific support through the product’s official support path.
- Manually review every AI-assisted post for truth, specificity, confidentiality, and platform fit. If automation materially produced an SEO resource in a way readers would reasonably care about, explain the process and human review; Google recommends making the “who, how, and why” of content clear ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content#ask-who-how-and-why-about-your-content)).

### Pre-publication check

Before any post, email, event, or partner asset goes live, confirm:

1. Is every capability claim true in the current production product?
2. Is the source universe and denominator clear?
3. Is all customer/prospect data synthetic, public with permission, or properly redacted?
4. Is the founder/product affiliation visible?
5. Is the CTA proportionate to the value given?
6. Is the UTM free of personal or confidential data?
7. Is any commercial email consent/basis recorded with identification and unsubscribe?
8. Does the destination page accurately describe trial, pricing, limits, privacy, and support?

## 10. Weekly Review and Open Questions

Every Friday, answer these questions in one page:

- Which problem statement generated qualified recruiter conversations?
- Which source produced activated workspaces rather than raw visits?
- Where did time to first value stall: target import, careers-source resolution, signal usefulness, contact context, or next action?
- Which claim or screen created a trust question?
- What did users do again without prompting?
- What should be stopped next week?

Open questions that could change this plan:

- Which recruiting niches have enough public ATS signal and repeatable roles to activate fastest?
- Is the free ATS audit the best acquisition asset, or does it attract operational researchers who do not need the paid workflow?
- Does the $10/month recruiter plan support the onboarding and support effort required for retention?
- Which commercial outcome—reply, meeting, opportunity, or time saved—best predicts renewal?
- Do activated customers want a peer community, a private office hour, or simply faster product support?
- Does first-touch or last-non-direct attribution better predict qualified activation for audit-assisted signups?
